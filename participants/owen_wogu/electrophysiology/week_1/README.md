What is a new fact you have learned about the brain?
    Before joining the fellowship, I had taken a few introductory 
    neuroscience courses, so some of the topics discussed in class 
    were already familiar to me. However, the lecture helped me see 
    neuroscience from a much broader perspective. I found it 
    especially interesting to learn about local field potentials 
    (LFPs), which showed me that studying the brain can go beyond EEG 
    signals and look at more localized brain activity. I also learned 
    that neuroscience is closely connected with genetics, allowing 
    researchers to study how genes can influence brain function and 
    behavior. After the lecture, I became curious about topics like 
    visual coding, which made me realize that neuroscience research 
    can also involve brain tissues, cells, genetics, and computational 
    methods. Overall, the class helped me understand that neuroscience 
    is a very broad field with many exciting areas to explore.

What questions arose when learning about the brain?
    The lecture answered many of my questions, but it also gave me new 
    research ideas. One question that stayed with me is whether 
    information from local field potential (LFP) studies could somehow 
    be used to improve the way we analyze EEG signals. Since there are 
    many open EEG datasets available for research, I wonder if 
    combining what we learn from LFPs with EEG analysis could help us 
    identify brain activity more accurately or improve the precision 
    of EEG-based studies. I know this may be a simple question, but it 
    is the kind of research question that makes me excited to keep 
    learning more about neuroscience and neuroengineering.
